## Is It Real? ##
You may have asked this question today, haven't you? I would guess that yes in case you watched some 'perfect' videos on the social medial you like the most. Well, if you asked yourself, I do not blame you. Specially with AI now, very likely questioning what is real will be around for a long time. 

This is one of the reasons why I decided to create this blog, to share real life experiences of a software engineer in a world where it seems there is no more reality, where everyone seems perfect, super efficient with no flaws building rocket-science projects. 

## The 'Sad' Reality ##
However, the truth is far from that. I usually think the real life of a software engineering is more like:

* To be in front of an empty canvas where engineers work as artists, working on the next painting filled with uncertainties but doing their best to make the least wrong decisions...

* Living in a jungle where we need to survive. It is at times exciting, at times overwhelming to be out there fighting for our survival. 

* Being in a maze that changes its routes every day forcing us constantly to learn new patterns.

Sounds negative? It shouldn't be.... aren't these challenges that boost us as engineers? Isn't it true that each challenge overcome and the lessons learnt in the process fills us with dopamine, bringing feelings of pleasure, motivation, even happiness? And for me, this should be the main focus of our energy.

The feelings that emerge  when we see the application or the solution we built running smoothly solving a real problem can be rewarding, and you know what? The words "running smoothly" are different from "running perfectly" which is the key point here. Countless people have been selling the idea of perfection in the IT industry resulting in a generation of engineers looking for such perfection in the code they write, in the solutions they bring, in the ideas they have or in the company they work for. Sad though, this thirsty for "perfection" has escalated to our lives, making many of us sick for it.

## Be Better not Perfect ##
The Pragmatic Engineer book quotes something that got me thinking for a while until I could completely digest that. The quotation is:

_"No one in the brief history of computing has ever written a piece of perfect software. It's unlikely that you'll be the first. And unless you accept this as a fact, you'll end up wasting time and energy chasing an impossible dream." - The Pragmatic Engineer_

It may sound hard to hear but yeah 
* __"THERE IS NO PERFECT CODE"__
* __"THERE IS NO PERFECT PROJECT"__
* __"THERE IS NO PERFECT COMPANY"__

And for now, it is better to accept it. Most of what is shown in the media is not real. We are humans, we are imperfect and make mistakes what doesn't mean we should passively accept it, rather it can mean we understand that everyone have flaws. For now, the treasure lives in trying to get better, learning, improving and growing as engineers, as humans. Such a balanced view has been helpful in many ways for me and I hope you can also take advantage of it.
